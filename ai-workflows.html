02 PROBLEM 1 OF 4 › WHERE CAN AI BE TRUSTED IN REAL RESEARCH?

Testing AI on a real study before asking the team to use it

The problem: Researchers had no evidence on where AI saves time and where it adds errors, and guessing wrong could put bad data in front of product teams.
What I did: I ran a national US survey (nearly 5,000 respondents) with AI assisting all 10 phases, then turned the lessons into a 36-slide playbook for fellow researchers.
Where AI saved real time (rated 4 to 5 out of 5) Where AI fell short (what I built safeguards for)
Survey setup. Built the full survey, including separate iPhone and Android paths, in minutes instead of hours. Overstated comparisons. Claimed older studies matched new results without checking how they differed. (Slides 3 and 5)
Pre-launch testing. Took the survey like a respondent on desktop and mobile and caught a hidden display bug. Inconsistent statistics. Changed table formats between sections and dropped effect sizes. (Slide 5)
Data cleaning. Compared quality filters side by side so I could keep iPhone and Android groups balanced. Overconfident writing. Defaulted to dense, passive prose and claims the data didn't support. (Slide 5)
Advanced analysis. Ran feature-priority models (MaxDiff and TURF) and subgroup comparisons with effect sizes. Lost reviewer feedback. Erased colleagues' comments and broke formatting when updating shared docs. (Slide 5)
Takeaway: AI reliably sped up the mechanical parts of a study, and its weak spots in synthesis and reporting set the roadmap for the shared tools that followed.

03 PROBLEM 2 OF 4 › PAST RESEARCH WAS HARD TO FIND AND TRUST

Making past research easy to find and safe to trust

The problem: Years of research sat buried in old decks and spreadsheets. Teams either spent days digging or used AI search, which treated old product pitches and opinions as if they were user evidence.
WHAT I DID
  • Built a synthesis tool with strict evidence rules. It draws on past UX studies, company announcements, hands-on press reviews, and public user discussions. Product specs, mockups, and opinions never count as user evidence.
  • Kept researchers in the loop. Every report lists the questions the evidence can't answer and names a UX researcher to talk to.
  • Improved our discovery tool through a pilot. PMs, designers, researchers, and data scientists flagged gaps. I added public user sentiment, restored older foundational studies, and kept analytics separate from user research.

04 PROBLEM 3 OF 4 › SURVEY SCORES DIDN'T EXPLAIN WHY

Turning in-app survey scores into answers teams can act on

The problem: In-app surveys showed whether satisfaction moved, but not why. Planning each one took days across research, product, engineering, and data science, and Google Messages (over 1 billion monthly users) couldn't link responses to how people used a feature.
1. PLAN
1-page plan in minutes
Best practices plus researcher sign-off
➜
2. CONNECT
Responses linked to feature use
Production app code I wrote and shipped
➜
3. ANALYZE
Results by real feature use
Compare feature users with non-users
WHAT I DID
  • Built a survey planning tool. It turns a feature idea or draft into a 1-page plan with research-backed question design, the right timing, and lessons from similar past surveys. It checks that the app records what the survey needs, and plans drafted by PMs or engineers require a UX researcher's sign-off.
  • Shipped the production code myself. Rather than wait quarters for engineering time, I used AI-assisted coding to update Google Messages so each survey response can carry product details, like which features someone uses. Android engineers and privacy reviewers approved it.
  • Built privacy in from the start. Responses aren't tied to anyone's account, people see what's shared, and nothing is sent unless they take the survey.
Takeaway: Teams can now go from a feature idea to survey results broken down by real feature use, without waiting quarters for engineering.

05 PROBLEM 4 OF 4 › AI DRAFTS WEREN'T RESEARCH-GRADE

Keeping AI-drafted research readouts clear and accurate

The problem: AI drafts reports in seconds, but the drafts often overstate findings, report statistics inconsistently, and bury the takeaway. On one survey readout, nearly half my follow-up requests went to fixing those issues.
WHAT I DID
  • Turned my editing habits into shared standards. I studied my own revision history and turned 25 recurring fixes into rules that two shared tools apply automatically.
  • Writing that reads like a senior researcher's. The writing tool ties each claim to the data and population studied, rewrites in plain active language, and ends with a fresh-eyes review.
  • Statistics anyone can read. Results state confidence in plain words, show effect sizes alongside, and flag groups too small to trust.
  • Colleagues' comments stay intact. Updates to shared documents keep reviewers' feedback in place.
WHAT CHANGED · ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE
BEFORE · TYPICAL AI DRAFT
"Users overwhelmingly prefer the new design, proving it will boost engagement."
AFTER · WITH SHARED STANDARDS
"More users preferred the new design. We're confident at the 95% level (p < .05), but the effect is small, so a follow-up test should check engagement."
36 to 18 words: My own edits halved the average summary sentence on a strategy paper. The tools now apply those same rules.
Takeaway: Shared writing and statistics tools automate the fixes that once took nearly half my follow-up requests and give junior researchers and partners senior standards by default.

Principle In practice Why it matters
1. Automate the mechanics, keep people as ground truth Let AI handle survey setup, testing, data cleaning, and archive searches around real participants. Teams save days of manual work without the risks of synthetic users.
2. Build rigor into shared tools Put evidence rules, survey best practices, and statistics standards into tools everyone uses. Junior researchers, PMs, and designers get senior-level guardrails by default.
3. Keep researchers in the loop Design self-serve tools to flag open questions and route teams to the right researcher. Partners drove 2/3 of synthesis sessions and still routed open questions to researchers.
4. Unblock measurement directly When a missing app change blocks research, ship it with engineering and privacy review. Teams get the data they need without waiting quarters.
Takeaway: Research teams can move at product speed and protect data quality when they pair mixed-methods rigor and team training with shared AI safeguards.
