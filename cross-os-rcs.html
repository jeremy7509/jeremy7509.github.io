00 PROGRAM OVERVIEW › GOOGLE MESSAGES UXR (2019 TO 2026)

Solving the cross-OS messaging divide and scaling RCS

Program overview: Since May 2019, I built and managed the Google Messages UX research team from 1 to 5+ researchers and led or directed 25+ mixed-methods studies on RCS and cross-OS group messaging. Our work progressed across three phases, from early SMS/MMS bridges to 1B+ Android RCS users and iOS 18 interoperability.
01 · DIAGNOSE & BRIDGE (2019–2021)

Cross-OS pain & lock-in

  • Separated teen bubble-color myths from concrete MMS media and group-thread bugs across US iPhone and Android users.
  • Priced iMessage lock-in against smartphone hardware via conjoint and shipped cross-OS video link and reaction bridges.
02 · SCALE TO 1B+ USERS (2021–2024)

OEM defaults & iOS 18 interop

  • Backed US carrier and global Samsung default deals, fixed OEM share-sheet icon confusion, and cut low-return chat hub bets.
  • Drove the "RCS chats" rebrand and default setup past 1B+ active users and shaped the advocacy behind Apple adopting RCS in iOS 18.
03 · EVALUATE & NEXT WAVE (2024–2026)

Post-launch impact & parity

  • Evaluated real-world iOS 18 RCS impact across a US cross-OS groups survey, dual-OS interviews, and MaxDiff prioritization.
  • Broke down in-product satisfaction by phone brand and user mix to separate cross-OS delivery gains from app-switcher headwinds.
Takeaway: Across seven years, 25+ mixed-methods studies guided cross-OS product bridges, global Android RCS scale past 1B+ users, and the post-iOS-18 parity roadmap.

01 PROBLEM, LOCK-IN & STAKEHOLDERS › AMBIGUITY, CONJOINT & AUDIENCES

Why the cross-OS messaging problem was initially unclear

Context: Teams knew mixed Android and iPhone group chats felt broken in the US. However, leaders lacked data on which failures mattered to everyday adults, how messaging lock-in compared to hardware upgrades, or why satisfaction softened after iOS 18 RCS launched.
PUBLIC MYTHS VS. MMS BUGS

Status tropes hid protocol bugs

  • Public commentary blamed teen bubble-color stigma, yet roughly half of surveyed US iPhone users did not even know what a green bubble meant.
  • Teams needed a prioritized view of compressed videos, split replies, static group rosters, and raw reaction texts to focus engineering.
ECOSYSTEM LOCK-IN & SCALE

Rivaled 3 hardware upgrades

  • In our US conjoint study, iPhone users valued iMessage and FaceTime on par with combining upgrades in display resolution, storage, and battery life.
  • US Android users split close contacts evenly across iOS and Android (leaving half on SMS/MMS). Dual OEM preloads and opt-in hurdles also slowed RCS scale.
FIVE DECISION AUDIENCES

Who needed empirical answers

  • Messages Product, Engineering, UX, and cross-product media partners needed clear feature priorities and sign-in-free cross-OS video playback.
  • Director- and VP-level leaders, Business Development, OEM/carrier partners, and #GetTheMessage marketing needed data to align defaults and advocacy.
Takeaway: We aligned five decision audiences by pricing iMessage lock-in against hardware upgrades and separating social myths from protocol and onboarding bugs.

03 SHIPPED IMPACT (2019–2024) › 1B+ USERS & IOS 18 INTEROP

What shipped to scale Android RCS and reach iOS 18 interop

From bridges to ecosystem scale: Insights from the 2019–2024 studies guided five years of Google Messages launches and backed global OEM and carrier default agreements past 1B+ monthly active users. These findings also shaped the advocacy that contributed to Apple adopting RCS in iOS 18.
01 · CROSS-OS PRODUCT BRIDGES

Shipped features & focus

  • Recipient link and reaction studies drove RCS emoji reactions, translated iMessage tapbacks, sign-in-free cloud video links, multi-contact sharing, and Scheduled Send.
  • Readiness interviews showed thread splits caused churn, so engineers kept SMS history inside upgraded iOS 18 RCS groups and cut chat hub and forced theme bets.
02 · TEAM, OEM & CARRIER SCALE

Scaled team & OEM defaults

  • I grew and managed Messages UXR from 1 to 5+ researchers. Conjoint findings also justified broader engineering staffing and US carrier default deals.
  • Sharing-menu A/B tests and lock-in data helped win Samsung and global OEM default agreements, fix share sheets, and sunset duplicate preloaded apps.
03 · BRAND, AUTO-ON & INTEROP

1B+ users & iOS 18 advocacy

  • Naming and onboarding studies drove the global "RCS chats" rebrand and default auto-on setup to help Android RCS surpass 1B+ monthly active users.
  • Cross-OS media, contact-mix, and social friction findings influenced VPs, SVPs, Samsung executives, and #GetTheMessage ahead of Apple iOS 18 RCS.
Takeaway: Shipped cross-OS bridges, global OEM and carrier defaults past 1B+ users, and empirical advocacy helped pave the way for iOS 18 RCS interoperability.

04 POST-LAUNCH EVALUATION (2024–2026) › IMPACT & NEXT ROADMAP

Evaluating post-iOS-18 impact and setting the next roadmap

Three-part post-launch program: After Apple launched RCS in iOS 18 (Sep 2024), I evaluated real-world impact through a US cross-OS groups survey, dual-OS interviews, and multi-year in-product satisfaction tracking.

Cross-OS groups survey measured gains and gaps: Nearly half of surveyed US adults said mixed iPhone and Android group chats improved over the prior year, especially photo and video sharing. Still, most hit occasional delivery or SMS fallback issues, and under half of Android users knew they could name a cross-OS group.

Interviews and MaxDiff defined next-wave parity: Dual-OS interviewees said sharper videos, typing indicators, and read receipts "closed the gap," and several mixed-OS families moved back from WhatsApp to default texting. In follow-up MaxDiff modeling, users selected two-way media quality, cross-OS video calling, and inline replies as most important far more often than bubble colors.

User-mix analysis resolved a top-line metric puzzle: In-product surveys showed fewer failed sends and more issue-free sessions across all Android brands. Breaking down scores by phone brand and user mix showed a slight US satisfaction dip came from reluctant users moved from discontinued apps. We focused the roadmap on parity, fallback recovery, and entry-level phone speed.

Takeaway: Post-launch research confirmed cross-OS delivery gains across every Android phone brand and separated out app-switcher headwinds.
